# Indian Fashion AI Evaluation

*One-Page Executive Summary | Premium Indian Fashion E-commerce*

## A. Evaluation objective

This evaluation compares three supplied AI-generated fashion images for premium Indian fashion and e-commerce use. It considers cultural authenticity, premium appeal, styling, visual quality, craftsmanship, and commercial usability—not only aesthetic attractiveness.

## B. Core setup

- **Models:** A — GPT-Image-2.5 / ChatGPT Image 2.5; B — Nano Banana 2.1; C — Nano Banana Pro.
- **AI evaluation:** Ten weighted criteria, integer scores from 0–10, and deterministic weighted aggregation.
- **Actual workload:** Three valid evaluations—one simulated-persona evaluation per image. The simulated-AI layer is not a group of real participants; ten independent personas were not evaluated.
- **Human evaluation:** Eight adults aged 21–24 supplied overall 1–5 star ratings and qualitative comments.

## C. Results at a glance

| Image/model | AI rank | AI score | Human rank | Human mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A — GPT-Image-2.5 | 1 | 8.89/10 | 1 | 4.50/5 |
| C — Nano Banana Pro | 2 | 8.52/10 | 2 | 3.625/5 |
| B — Nano Banana 2.1 | 3 | 8.35/10 | 3 | 3.125/5 |

The AI evaluator and human participants produced the same descriptive ranking in this run and sample; this does not establish statistical agreement or general model superiority. Human totals were A = 36/40, C = 29/40, and B = 25/40. AI scores and human ratings remain separate measures.

## D. Main qualitative findings

- **A — Premium campaign:** Heritage-led storytelling, craftsmanship, coordinated styling, festive relevance, and premium composition.
- **B — Everyday wearability:** Contemporary Indian identity, approachable styling, and potential for everyday ethnic-wear listings. Some participants preferred it for practical wear despite its lower mean.
- **C — Editorial sophistication:** Graceful styling, polished presentation, typography, and a clear shopping message. Some found its setting and palette less distinctly Indian.

These are themes from the supplied responses; they do not imply that every participant agreed with every theme.

## E. Most important takeaway

**The best image depends on the commercial placement, not just the overall score.** Image A appears most promising for premium heritage-led campaigns, Image B may suit everyday ethnic-wear listings, and Image C may fit sophisticated editorial or brand-awareness creative. These are preliminary placement hypotheses, not validated conversion outcomes.

## F. Key limitations

- Only one image was evaluated for each labelled model.
- One valid simulated-persona evaluation was completed per image.
- The human sample contained eight participants; representativeness is not established.
- Human ratings were overall 1–5 ratings, not criterion-level scores.
- P7 and P8 provided identical qualitative wording in the supplied data; this requires verification.
- Model provenance and the original image-generation brief were not independently verified in the available evidence.
